# Derja and Arabizi Mixing Strategy

This document defines the compositional breakdown for Derja and Arabizi content within the training data mixture. No specific datasets are referenced here; this is a strategic allocation plan only.

## Current strategy

Gemma Tounsi 1.0 prioritizes natural, fluent, everyday Tunisian communication. Both the `arabizi` and `arabic_derja` slices are 100% ordinary Tunisian content — there is no dedicated mathematics, coding, or STEM sub-allocation within either slice.

## Arabizi (35% total)

Of the 35% allocated to Arabizi content:

- **35%** — Regular Arabizi: everyday conversational and informal Tunisian text written in Latin script with numeric markers (e.g., 3, 7, 5, 9). Covers daily life, culture, relationships, food, travel, education, personal experiences, and general knowledge — all in natural Arabizi.

## Derja (25% total)

Of the 25% allocated to Derja content:

- **25%** — Regular Derja: everyday conversational and informal Tunisian text written in Arabic script. Covers daily life, culture, relationships, food, travel, education, personal experiences, and general knowledge — all in natural Derja.

## Summary

| Script | Share of Total | Subcategory | Share of Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arabizi | 35% | Regular Arabizi (ordinary Tunisian content) | 35% |
| Derja | 25% | Regular Derja (ordinary Tunisian content) | 25% |

## Superseded strategy

The previous strategy allocated a portion of each slice to specialized technical domains (mathematics, programming, technical problem-solving). That requirement has been **permanently dropped**. The data budget previously reserved for Math-and-Coding-Arabizi (15% of the cocktail) and Math-and-Coding-Derja (10% of the cocktail) has been reallocated to ordinary Arabizi and Derja content respectively, preserving each slice's total share. See git history for the superseded allocation.

## Desired content examples

- Natural everyday conversations
- Questions and answers about daily life
- Tunisian culture, traditions, and social situations
- Personal experiences, storytelling, and opinions
- Education and student life
- Relationships, family, and social interactions
- Travel, food, shopping, and local situations
- Explanations of ordinary subjects in natural Tunisian language
- General knowledge and practical advice
- Informal discussions and realistic multi-turn conversations
